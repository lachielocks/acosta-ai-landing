There's something special about education.

It's that moment when something you've struggled to understand finally makes sense. It's the excitement of discovering something you never knew existed. It's asking a question, finding an answer, and somehow ending up with ten more questions.

And behind so many of those moments are teachers.

The teachers who made learning fun. The teachers who made a subject you never thought you'd enjoy become one of your favourites. The teachers who encouraged you to ask questions, even when those questions had absolutely nothing to do with the lesson. The teachers who made you laugh, challenged the way you thought and saw potential in you before you saw it in yourself.

They might not remember every lesson they've taught, but their students will remember how those lessons made them feel.

That's the kind of impact that extends far beyond a classroom.

And that's a big part of why I created Acosta.

I wanted to build something that celebrates curiosity. Something that makes learning a little more accessible, a little more enjoyable and a little less intimidating. Not something that replaces the people who make education special, but something that complements what they do.

Acosta AI was founded, designed and developed independently by me, Lachie Thurlow. But whilst I'm the only person building it, I'm certainly not the only reason it exists.

Every lesson, every conversation, every friendship, every bit of encouragement, every mistake and every wonderfully ridiculous idea has helped shape the person behind Acosta and, in turn, Acosta itself.

Some of the people who have inspired me probably don't even realise they have. Some may never read this. But that doesn't make their influence any less meaningful.

I think we could all spend a little more time thanking the people who have helped us become who we are.

Every time someone uses Acosta, shares an idea, reports a bug, asks a question or simply tells me they like what I'm building, it reminds me why I started.

I'm the founder, developer, designer, product manager, customer support department and the person responsible for fixing whatever the developer broke.

The developer and I are currently on speaking terms.

I'm still figuring things out. I'm still learning. Acosta is still growing. There will be mistakes, changes, better ideas and probably a few more questionable dad jokes along the way.

You don't have to wait until you've finished school to start creating something. You don't have to know everything before you begin. You don't need to have it all figured out.
